About Me

Hi, I'm Meredith Stone! I'm currently a fourth-year PhD candidate in Astronomy and Astrophysics at the University of Arizona, intending to defend in the spring/summer of 2027.

My graduate work has primarily used the James Webb Space Telescope, but I'm broadly interested in studying galaxies in the rest-UV to mm to understand how secular processes like star formation and AGN activity, together with galaxy mergers, work together over cosmic time to produce the diversity of galaxies we observe in the local Universe.

Research

I use the James Webb Space Telescope to study the host galaxies and environments of bright high-redshift quasars to understand their future evolution and how they relate to galaxies in the local Universe.

At lower redshift, I'm currently studying the interstellar conditions of main-sequence galaxies near cosmic noon using PAH emission features. JWST observations now allow us, for the first time, to study PAHs in statistical samples of typical galaxies outside the local Universe.

Previously, I've calculated galaxy number counts in the SMILES survey to constrain cosmological models, and created stacks of Spitzer Space Telescope spectra to develop mid-infrared AGN and star formation diagnostics applicable to higher redshift with PRIMA.

Teaching and Outreach

I'm passionate about encouraging K-12 participation in STEM through my work with Arizona Astronomy Camp and the Southern AZ Regional Science Fair. At the undergraduate level, I'm a mentor for the TIMESTEP program at the University of Arizona, where I help guide students through their first research experiences. By teaching our undergraduate students critical research skills early in their degree, we help make research participation—and with that, research careers within and outside academia—more transparent and accessible to all.
